This STANDARD is NOT MET as evidenced by:

Based on record review and interview, the facility
failed to ensure a restrictive Behavior Support Plan
(BSP) was conducted with the written consent of all
legal guardians. This affected 1 of 3 audit clients
(#5). The finding is:

Record review on 6/16/26 of client #5's BSP written
on 8/12/25 revealed client #5 was on a behavior
management plan that included psychotropic
medications for required relaxation. The guardian
signed the consent on 10/8/25 that he approved of
the current BSP. On 2/24/26 at the Mini-Team
meeting revealed client #5 would start wearing a
two-finger glove to help control picking his thumb.
On 3/4/26 the Behavior Specialist recorded she
gathered a verbal consent from the guardian to
make changes to client #5's BSP. There was no
record that the facility acquired a written consent
from the guardian on the amended BSP for client #5.

Record review on 6/16/26 revealed the Qualified
Intellectual Disabilities Professional (QIDP) wrote in a
progress note that client #5's guardian visited the
home in May 2026. There was no record that the
guardian signed the amended BSP consent for client
#5 during his visit.

Interview on 6/16/26 with the Behavior Specialist,

she acknowledged they did not have a written BSP
consent from client #5's guardian.

This STANDARD is NOT MET as evidenced by:

Based on record review and interviews, the facility
failed to ensure that laboratory results were finalized
and reviewed for irregularities. This affected 1 of 3
audit clients (#1). The finding is:

Record review on 6/15/26 of client #1’s Physician’s
Orders signed on 4/7/26 revealed he was diagnosed
with Epilepsy and took Clonazepam, Gabapentin,
Lacosamide, Levetiracetam and Phenytoin EX for
seizures,

Record review on 6/15/26 of client #1's laboratory
partial report on 4/13/26 revealed blood collected on
4/11/26 still had pending results for Keppra and
Lacosamide. The quarterly nursing assessment on
6/9/26 did not identify that all laboratory results
were still outstanding.

Interview on 6/16/26 with the Licensed Practical
Nurse (LPN) revealed the laboratory will contact the
nurse if the client's results are critical. The LPN did
not have the final results of the labs but would
download the results from the laboratory. The LPN
presented an updated lab result from the blood
drawn for client #1 on 4/11/26 that revealed the
Keppra was high at 56.5. The normal range is listed
at 10.0-40.0. The LPN acknowledged if she saw the
elevated Keppra levels in April, she would have
contacted client #1’s Neurologist to inform him and
learn if the doctor wanted to titrate the dosage of
medication. The LPN also confirmed that client #1
has had no seizure activity.

Interview on 6/16/26 with the Registered Nurse (RN)
revealed after laboratory results are completed for
clients, they wait until the doctor reviews them
before putting them on the chart. The RN revealed it
was rare for the laboratory to forget to send them
the results and typically they are contacted if the
client had a critical lab after testing. The RN
acknowledged because the results for Keppra had
not been sent to the facility for client #1, the
protocol included to retake them and notify the
Neurologist if the levels are critical.

Based on observation, policy review and interviews,
the facility failed to ensure emergency provisions for
subsistence for staff and clients included adequate

revealed there were two large plastic totes in the
first pantry in the kitchen, with opened padlock. The
bottom tote was empty, and the top tote had many
small jars of baby food and a box of nonfat powder
milk. A jar of meatballs baby food had an expiration
date of April 2025. An additional observation of the
2nd pantry in the kitchen revealed there were no
containers of water stored.

3/24/26 revealed if a product has not been used by
this (expiration) date, throw it away.

the reason they purchased baby food.

(QA) Manager revealed he learned from the group
home staff that water was consumed during a hot
weather spell and had not been replaced.

INDIVIDUAL PROGRAM PLAN

CFR(s): 483.440(c)(3)(v)

The comprehensive functional assessment must
include adaptive behaviors or independent living
skills necessary for the client to be able to function
in the community.

This STANDARD is NOT MET as evidenced by:

Based on observations, record review and
interviews, the facility failed to ensure client #2’s
Comprehensive Functional Assessment (CFA)
included an assessment of her dining skills. This
affected 1 of 3 audit clients. The finding is:

During evening observations in the home on
7/27/28 at 5:47pm, client #2’s food items were
placed on her plate and drinks were poured while
she was in the medication room to receive her
medications. The client was not prompted or
encouraged to participate with serving herself or
pouring her drinks.

Interview on 7/27/26 with Staff B revealed they used
to have clients participate in family style dining
about five years ago, however, they don’t do that
anymore. Additional interview indicated the staff
prepare client's plates at the kitchen counter and
will take it too them at the table.

Review on 7/28/26 of client #2’s Daily Living
Functional Assessment (DLFA) dated 4/11/24 did not
include an assessment of her dining skills.

Interview on 7/28/26 with the Qualified Intellectual
Disabilities Professional (QIDP) revealed she thought
the clients use to participate in family style dining
tasks years ago, however, she was not working at
the facility at that time. The QIDP confirmed client
#2’s DLFA does not include her dining skills.

This STANDARD is NOT MET as evidenced by:

Based on observations, record review and
interviews, the facility failed to ensure all client’s
received their specially modified diets as ordered.
This affected 1 of 3 audit clients (#14). The finding
is:

During dinner observations in the home on 7/27/26
at 5:52pm, client #14’s meatloaf was ground and
dry. During additional breakfast observations on
7/28/26 at 7:10am, client #14’s sausage was
ground, moist with liquid surrounding it on her plate.
At both meals, the client consumed the meats
without difficulty.

Interview on 7/28/26 with Staff D revealed client #14
receives pureed meats with other foods ground and
moistened. Additional interview indicated pureed
meats should be “like baby food”.

Review of a food texture list posted on the
refrigerator in the home revealed pureed foods
should be “blended smooth”. Additional review of
client #14’s Individual Program Plan (IPP) dated
9/30/25, her Occupational Therapy evaluation dated
2/3/26 and her physician’s orders signed 6/10/26
indicated the client receives pureed meats with other
foods ground and moistened.

Interview on 7/28/26 with the Qualified Intellectual
Disabilities Professional (QIDP) confirmed client #14
consumes her meats at a pureed texture. Additional
interview confirmed pureed foods should not have
chunks, be very smooth and look like baby food or
pudding.
